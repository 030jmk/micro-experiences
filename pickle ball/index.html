🏓 The Basics ▼

Pickleball is played on a badminton-sized court (6.1 × 13.4 m) with a lowered net (86 cm at the centre). Two or four players use solid paddles to hit a perforated plastic ball over the net.

Singles or Doubles Indoor or Outdoor All ages & abilities
  • The ball must clear the net and land in bounds.
  • Play continues until a fault is committed.
  • Only the serving side can score (side-out scoring).
🚀 Serving Rules ▼
  • The serve must be underhand, with the paddle below the wrist at contact.
  • The ball must be hit diagonally into the opposite service box.
  • The serve must clear the Non-Volley Zone (kitchen) — landing in it is a fault.
  • In doubles, both players on a team serve before the serve passes to the other team (except at the very start — only one serve is allowed on the first service turn).
  • The server's score determines which side they serve from: even score = right side, odd score = left side.
💡 In doubles, the score is called as three numbers: Server score – Receiver score – Server number (1 or 2). e.g. "4 – 2 – 1"

  • Side-out scoring (traditional): only the serving team scores. Losing a rally means losing the serve.
  • Rally scoring: a point is awarded on every rally regardless of who served — faster games.
  • A match is typically best of 3 games; finals are sometimes best of 5.
💡 Games are won at 11, but you must lead by at least 2. If it's 10–10, play continues until someone leads by 2 (e.g. 12–10).
🍳 The Kitchen (NVZ) ▼

The Non-Volley Zone (NVZ), nicknamed "the kitchen", is the 2.1 m area on each side of the net.

  • You cannot volley (hit the ball in the air) while standing in or touching the kitchen line.
  • You can enter the kitchen to play a ball that has bounced.
  • You must exit the kitchen before volleying again.
  • Momentum that carries you into the kitchen after a volley is also a fault — even if you've already hit the ball.
💡 "Dinking" — soft, angled shots that land in the opponent's kitchen — is a key tactical skill. It forces opponents into the kitchen and sets up winning volleys.
⬆️ Double Bounce Rule ▼

After the serve, each side must let the ball bounce once before hitting it:

  • The receiving team must let the serve bounce before returning it.
  • The serving team must then let the return bounce before playing it.
  • After those two bounces, either team may volley or play off the bounce.
💡 This rule prevents serve-and-volley dominance and encourages longer rallies. It's sometimes called the "two-bounce rule".
⚠️ Faults ▼

A fault ends the rally. Common faults include:

  • Ball lands out of bounds
  • Ball hits the net
  • Volleying from the kitchen or kitchen line
  • Violating the double bounce rule
  • Serve lands in the kitchen or out of the service box
  • Ball bounces twice before being returned
  • Player or clothing touches the net
